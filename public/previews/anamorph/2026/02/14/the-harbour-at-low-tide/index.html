The harbour at low tide

14 February 2026 series, stills, books

  • Camera: 6x7 rangefinder, 80mm
  • Film: Tri-X, developed in the studio
  • Prints: silver gelatin, 50 x 60 cm
  • Book: Tidewater Press, 2026

Twice a day the water leaves, and the harbour shows you its bones: the chains, the old tyres hung as fenders, the steps worn into a bowl by two hundred years of boots. For six winters I stood at the same wall at the lowest water of each month and made one exposure.

January. The outer harbour, fog lifting at eleven.

February. The boy who sells bait, walking out to the second mooring post.

What the mud keeps

A spoon, a bicycle bell, the brass plate from a boat that sank before anyone now living was born. The mud keeps everything and gives it back slowly.

The harbour is not a place. It is a schedule that happens to have walls.

March. The old pier at dusk, the last frame on the roll.

The book collects forty-eight of these plates, one for each month, in the order they were made.
