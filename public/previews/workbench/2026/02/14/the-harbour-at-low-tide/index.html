The harbour at low tide, as a dataset

What a year of low-water measurements taught me about the harbour, and about my own code.

Twice a day the water leaves and the harbour shows its bones. For a year I logged every low tide with a cheap sensor on the second mooring post.

The readings disagreed with my own predictions by up to eleven minutes. The fault was mine: a rounding error that had lived in the code for three years. The fix was one line; finding it took a winter.
