The harbour at low tide

Twice a day the water leaves and the town shows you how it is held together.

14 February 2026

  • essays
  • places

Twice a day the water leaves, and the harbour shows you its bones: the chains, the old tyres hung as fenders, the steps worn into a bowl by two hundred years of boots.

At low tide you can walk out to the second mooring post. Nobody tells you this. You learn it by watching the boy who sells bait, who learned it from someone else.

What the mud keeps

A spoon, a bicycle bell, the brass plate from a boat that sank before anyone now living was born. The mud keeps everything and gives it back slowly.

The harbour is not a place. It is a schedule that happens to have walls.

I go down most evenings now. The light is better than any photograph of it.
